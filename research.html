02 / Lessons from real incidents

Fire safety is a system problem.

Historical cases provide design context. They are not controlled experiments or datasets for calibrating the simulator.

Incident → outcome → design response
CaseWhat happenedReported outcomeLesson / response
Apollo 1 · 1967 ↗A ground-test cabin fire occurred in a pure-oxygen atmosphere.Three crew members died. Rising pressure prevented opening the inward hatch.Combustible materials were restricted; a unified quick-opening hatch and more fire-resistant suits followed.
Apollo 13 · 1970 ↗An oxygen-tank failure damaged critical spacecraft systems.The lunar landing was abandoned and the crew returned home.Oxygen-system redesign, recertification, electrical protection, and additional redundancy followed. This was not a cabin flame experiment.
Mir · 1997 ↗An oxygen-generator canister fire obstructed one escape route and filled modules with smoke.The fire ended without structural damage or lasting crew harm; smoke clearance took hours.Consider evacuation access, respiratory protection, equipment placement, and post-fire atmosphere recovery together.
